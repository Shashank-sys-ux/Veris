## A0. Headline for the deck

The single most important finding in Section A is **A5**: Zurich already ships an AI tool that reads policy wording and flags coverage gaps and sublimit mismatches. ## A5. What already exists at Zurich that could make your idea redundant — **MOST IMPORTANT SECTION**

Ranked by redundancy risk, highest first.

### 1. Zurich Program IQ — direct overlap on mechanic, different segment [VERIFIED]

Launched **December 2025**. An AI-enabled policy review system.
- **What it does:** built in-house for Zurich's underwriting teams; **scans sublimits across local policies and the master policy and flags mismatches**. Zurich's stated rationale: those differences can create unseen exposures, **open coverage gaps**, and add operational drag. Early detection allows correction **before bind or renewal**.
- **Current scope:** property natural catastrophe within multinational programmes; processes policies in **multiple languages**; Zurich states additional lines will follow.
- **Scale context given by Zurich:** its multinational network spans more than 200 countries and territories, supporting over **7,500 customers** through approximately **8,500 insurance programmes**.
- Quoted: **Sierra Signorelli, CEO Commercial Insurance** — on delivering clarity and consistency across markets, and on reviewing thousands of pages of policy wording to give customers greater confidence in programme coherence.
- Sources: [FinTech Global, 22 Dec 2025](https://fintech.global/2025/12/22/zurich-program-iq-applies-ai-to-global-insurance-contracts/); [Complete AI Training, "Zurich launches Program IQ"](https://completeaitraining.com/news/zurich-launches-program-iq-an-ai-tool-that-flags-policy/) which cites Zurich Insurance Group as source and dates the launch 31 December 2025. Both are **[SECONDARY]** — I did not locate the zurich.com press release itself, so attribute to the trade outlet.

**Why this is your biggest risk and your biggest asset.**
- Risk: a Zurich judge can say "we already built an AI tool that reads policies and flags coverage gaps."

# SECTION B — SINGAPORE REGULATORY PERIMETER

## B0. The one-paragraph answer

A software tool that reads an SME's own general insurance documents and states factually what is and is not covered sits, on the evidence I found, **outside** the Financial Advisers Act perimeter — because the FAA regulates advice on *investment products*, and general insurance (property, liability, work injury, motor) is not one. It sits **outside** the Insurance Act intermediary regime provided the tool itself does not arrange or solicit contracts of insurance. The binding constraints on you are therefore **not** licensing. They are: **PDPA** (you will ingest employee and director personal data), **MAS third-party / technology risk expectations flowing down from Zurich as your customer**, and **MAS's AI risk management expectations**, which are not law but which Zurich will apply to you contractually. Get those three right and you will look more prepared than most incumbents.

**[INFERENCE]** This is a genuinely strong position and you should lead with it, because most teams will either ignore the perimeter or over-claim that they need a licence. The judges — insurance and financial services experts — will notice which.

**[IMPORTANT]** This is research, not legal advice. Every conclusion below should be confirmed with Singapore counsel before you make any representation to MAS or Zurich about your regulatory status.

## B1. When does telling a Singapore business about gaps in its own policy become regulated activity?

### The four-way split in Singapore's intermediary regime [VERIFIED]

This is the framework, and the brief's assumption about GIA is **correct**. Confirmed:

| Who | What they do | Authorisation needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Insurance agents (general insurance only)** acting **on behalf of insurers** | Arrange general insurance products for insurers | **No licence.** Must be **registered with the General Insurance Association of Singapore (GIA)** |
| **Insurance brokers** acting **for policyholders** | Arrange **general** and **long-term accident & health** policies for insureds | **Registered as an insurance broker with MAS under the Insurance Act**, or registered as an **exempt insurance broker** if eligible |
| **Anyone distributing or advising on life products** (whether for insurer or policyholder) | Life policies | **Financial adviser's licence under the FAA**, or registration with MAS as an **exempt financial adviser** if eligible |
| **Individual representatives** | Sale or advice on insurance | Minimum qualification and examination requirements by product type |

- Source: [Baker McKenzie Resource Hub — Guide for Insurance Sales, Advisory and Distribution, Singapore](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/asia-pacific-insurance/asia-pacific/singapore/topics/guide-for-insurance-sales-advisory-and-distribution).

**Verdict on the brief's proposition: CONFIRMED.** Insurance agents who arrange general insurance products only, on behalf of insurers, do not need a licence but must be registered with the GIA. That is exactly right.

**Three refinements you should add so the statement is precise:**
1. It is **brokers**, not agents, who go to **MAS** — brokers acting for the *policyholder* register under the **Insurance Act**. The brokered scope explicitly includes **long-term accident & health** as well as general.
2. The **FAA** hook is **life products** and investment products, not "insurance" generally. The moment anything you do touches a life policy, the analysis changes completely.
3. The GIA registration route requires representatives to hold the **Certificate in General Insurance (CGI)** qualification, and the **health insurance module** from the Singapore College of Insurance for health products; life products require **CMFAS Modules 5, 9 and 9A**. Source: same Baker McKenzie guide. Corroborating on the examination structure and the GIA agent registration paperwork: [AIG Singapore corporate agency application form](https://orgn-aigsg1.dmp.aig.com/content/dam/aig/apac/singapore/documents/forms/aig_agency_application_corporate.pdf.coredownload.pdf), which references GIA Form A / Form C and the CGI / BCP / PGI / ComGI and HI examination requirements.

### Is a software tool used by a licensed or registered intermediary outside the perimeter?

**[INFERENCE — reasoned, not sourced. Flag it as inference in any deck.]**

The regulated act is *advising* or *arranging*. Where the tool is operated by, and its output is issued under the name and responsibility of, a GIA-registered agent or a MAS-registered broker, the regulated person is the intermediary, and your product is an input into their process. That is the same structure as an underwriting workbench, a rating engine or a comparison sheet — none of which are separately licensed.

Two things break that analysis:
1. **If the tool speaks directly to the SME and makes a recommendation** ("you should buy X"), you are no longer an input; you are potentially advising. Whether that matters depends entirely on the FAA "investment product" boundary above — for pure general insurance it likely does not, for anything touching life or health it very likely does.
2. **If the tool arranges or solicits** — collects an application, transmits it to an insurer, earns commission — you have crossed into the intermediary regime regardless of product class.

**Practical design recommendation.** Ship a **diagnostic that outputs findings, not recommendations**, and route the intermediary or the insurer to make the recommendation. Two concrete design rules:
- Output *"your policy's business interruption indemnity period is 12 months; your stated supplier lead time is 9 months and your fit-out lead time is 7 months"* — a fact plus a fact.
- Do not output *"you should extend your indemnity period to 24 months and buy from Zurich."*
This is not a cosmetic distinction; it is the perimeter.

### Is a factual statement about a document the client already owns "advice"?

**[INFERENCE — no Singapore authority found directly on point.]**

Reading back what a document says is a factual assertion, not a recommendation, and on any ordinary reading is not financial advice. **But** the distinction erodes fast in practice, and MAS has form here: the MAS Financial Advisory Guidelines were specifically designed to give clarity on when a person is deemed to be carrying on the business of financial advisory services, and were noted at the time as **particularly relevant to online activities, digital business models, and software and aggregator tools.**
- Source: [Lexology summary of MAS's proposed Financial Advisory Guidelines](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=4d1b2eab-85a9-402b-b1a8-314067b1b696), June 2018; [Baker McKenzie client alert on the same](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/-/media/files/insight/publications/2018/06/al_singapore_masproposesnewguidelines_june2018.pdf), June 2018, which notes MAS provided clarity on activities including portfolio allocation services provided by robo-advisory websites.

**[INFERENCE] What this means for you.** MAS has explicitly turned its mind to software and aggregator tools in the advisory perimeter. Do not assume "we're just a tool" is a defence. The safe zone is: factual extraction + factual comparison against an objective external reference (a statute, a lease clause, a stated lead time) + no product recommendation. The moment you rank or recommend products, get an opinion.

**Line to use with judges, which is both honest and confident:**
> *"For general insurance, on the advice we have, this sits outside the FAA. We have designed to the safe side of the line anyway: we output findings, an intermediary or insurer makes the recommendation. That's a product decision, not a limitation."*

### The specific expectations you can name, by area [VERIFIED]

**Governance and oversight**
- Cross-functional **AI oversight forums** spanning data and analytics, risk, legal and compliance, technology, audit and business functions.
- Policies and procedures updated for AI, in several cases consolidated into a **central AI guide**.
- Central statements and **principles for responsible use** — fair, ethical, accountable, transparent — with some firms mapping principles to specific controls and owning functions.
- AI capability building: staff and senior executive training; **AI Centres of Excellence**.

**Key risk management systems and processes**
- **AI identification** — extending existing model risk definitions to capture AI-specific characteristics; some firms built tools or portals to classify AI consistently.
- **Comprehensive AI inventory** — a formal record of where AI is used, used to track lifecycle, set risk-process checkpoints, monitor aggregate risk and interdependencies, and act as a repository for AI artefacts. Clear policies on scope, ownership and update process.
- **Risk materiality assessment** across three dimensions:
  1. **impact** on the firm, its customers and stakeholders;
  2. **complexity** of the model or **novelty** of the use case;
  3. **reliance** on the AI, or **autonomy** granted to it.

**Development and deployment**
- Updated MRM standards for development, validation, deployment, monitoring, change management. Baseline standards for all AI; **enhanced standards for higher risk materiality**.
- **Data management**: appropriateness of data for the use case, representativeness, robust data engineering and pipelines, documentation for reproducibility and auditability.
- **Model selection**: developers must justify choosing a more complex model, sometimes by building **challenger models** to demonstrate performance uplift.
- **Robustness and stability testing**: sensitivity analysis, stability analysis, sub-population analysis, error analysis, stress testing, with **defined and documented thresholds agreed between developers and validators**. Overfitting mitigants including favouring lower complexity and out-of-sample testing.
- **Explainability**: global and/or local explainability methods required; some firms define minimum explainability by use case.
- **Fairness**: define protected features; determine whether they were used in training; where necessary assess whether decisions systematically disadvantage at-risk groups; justify use of protected features.
- **Reproducibility and auditability**: expanded documentation using MRM-designed templates.
- **Independent validation**: required for higher-risk-materiality AI; peer review for the rest.
- **Deployment and monitoring**: pre-deployment production controls based on existing technology risk guidelines, plus forward testing and live edge-case testing; post-deployment ongoing monitoring of robustness, stability, data quality and fairness against tiered thresholds; issue and incident reporting and resolution; **contingency plans with fallback to alternative systems or manual processes**; **kill-switches for mission-critical applications**; periodic portfolio review and revalidation; approval by control functions for material changes; **version control including changes from third-party AI providers**; enhanced change-management controls for **"dynamic AI"** that updates automatically.

**Generative AI** (additional considerations)
- Named risks: **hallucinations and unexpected behaviour**, testing difficulty, **lack of transparency from GenAI providers** creating explainability challenges, and fairness.
- Practices: start with **limited use cases** rather than direct customer-facing deployment without a **human in the loop**; time- and user-bounded pilot policies; cross-functional control checks at lifecycle stages; developmental and validation guidelines by **GenAI task archetype**; extensive user education on limitations; standalone, functional and end-to-end assessments; use-case-specific testing datasets; **model vulnerability testing**; **input and output guardrails / filters**, some reusable; data security via private cloud or on-premise open-source models, contractual protections, DLP tooling and data-classification limits; exploration of **grounding methods**.

**Third-party AI** (this is you, from Zurich's perspective)
- Risks named: **unknown biases from pre-training data**, data protection concerns, **concentration risk** from interdependencies.
- Expected mitigants:
  - **rigorous compensatory testing** to verify robustness and stability and to detect bias;
  - **robust contingency plans** including backup systems or manual processes;
  - **contractual clauses** on performance guarantees, data protection, **audit rights**, and **notification or approval requirements when the provider introduces AI into an existing solution**;
  - staff training on AI literacy and risk awareness.

MAS's closing position: FIs should take reference from these practices, regularly update AI MRM frameworks, and also review non-AI-specific areas — general data governance and technology and cyber risk management — to account for AI developments. MAS signalled it planned to build on these focus areas and issue supervisory guidance for all FIs.

## B3. MAS Guidelines on the Provision of Digital Advisory Services — read-across

### What they are [VERIFIED]

MAS consulted in 2017 on facilitating the provision of digital advisory services and finalised guidelines in 2018. The core position:
- Digital advisers, also called **robo-advisers**, provide digital advisory services **using client-facing tools**.
- A digital adviser will **generally be regarded as carrying on financial advisory services under the Financial Advisers Act**; depending on the operating model, licensing under the **Securities and Futures Act** for fund management or dealing in capital markets products may also be required.

### Read-across to a general insurance diagnostic tool

**[INFERENCE — this is analysis, not a sourced conclusion.]**

**Direct application: unlikely.** The Digital Advisory Guidelines are framed around **investment products** and sit on the FAA / SFA licensing hooks. A general insurance coverage diagnostic advises on neither capital markets products nor life policies. On the FAA analysis in B1, the guidelines should not bite directly.

**Indirect application: near-certain, and you should treat it as binding.** Three reasons:
1. **It is the only MAS document that tells you what MAS thinks good looks like for a client-facing algorithmic advisory tool.** Whatever MAS expects of a robo-adviser's algorithm governance, MAS's supervisors, and Zurich's compliance function, will expect of yours by analogy.
2. **The technology-risk safeguards limb is product-agnostic.** MAS's requirement to manage risks arising from algorithms and online tools is not tied to product class.
3. **Reputational and supervisory reality.** If your tool tells 10,000 Singapore SMEs something wrong about their insurance, "we weren't technically in scope" will not be a satisfying answer to MAS.

**Recommendation.** Adopt the digital advisory safeguards **voluntarily and say so**: documented algorithm design and rationale; testing and validation of the logic before deployment; ongoing monitoring for logic drift; human review of edge cases; audit trail of what the tool told each customer and why; a documented escalation path when the tool cannot reach a confident conclusion. **[INFERENCE]** Presenting yourself as voluntarily meeting a standard you are not caught by is one of the strongest credibility moves available to a pre-incorporation team in front of financial services judges.

## B5. PDPA obligations when ingesting SME insurance documents

### Why this is the binding constraint, not licensing

**[INFERENCE]** A work injury compensation schedule contains employee names and occupations. A motor policy contains named drivers. A directors' and officers' policy and most commercial proposal forms contain director names and NRIC-adjacent identifiers. A group health schedule contains dates of birth and sometimes medical information. **The moment you accept an SME's document bundle, you are processing personal data of identifiable individuals who are not your customer and who have not spoken to you.** That is the real regulatory exposure of this product, and it is under-appreciated. Handle it explicitly in your submission.

### The statutory framework [VERIFIED]

**Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (PDPA)** — No. 26 of 2012.

**Personal data** is defined as data, whether true or not, about an individual who can be identified from that data, or from that data plus other information to which the organisation has or is likely to have access.
- Source (definition as commonly reproduced in PDPA-compliant privacy policies): [Banyan Tree data protection policy](https://www.banyantree.com/privacy-policy). **[SECONDARY]** — a private policy is not the statute. Confirm the statutory wording with counsel.

**Data protection obligations.** Organisations are responsible for personal data in their possession or under their control and must comply with the obligations when collecting, using or disclosing personal data. A specific constraint: an organisation may not, as a condition of providing a product or service, require an individual to consent to collection, use or disclosure of personal data **beyond what is reasonable to provide that product or service**.
- Sources: [PDPC — Data Protection Obligations](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/Overview-of-PDPA/The-Legislation/Personal-Data-Protection-Act/Data-Protection-Obligations); [PDPC — Data protection obligations under the PDPA (infographic)](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/resource-for-organisation/data-protection-obligations-under-the-pdpa.pdf).

**[INFERENCE] Direct product consequence.** You cannot make "upload every document including your full employee census" a condition of running a diagnostic if the diagnostic does not need names. **Design for redaction at ingestion:** if you only need headcount, occupational class and wage roll to test WICA adequacy, strip the names before they hit your model. This is both compliant and a competitive feature — it is the answer to "why would an SME trust you with their HR data."

### Consent and purpose limitation [VERIFIED, with the insurance-specific gloss]

In the insurance context specifically: insurers, agents and brokers must **notify clients of the purpose for which personal data is collected and obtain consent** for use and disclosure. Where an insurer receives client personal data through an intermediary or vice versa, **the recipient should take steps to ensure notification to and consent from the client have been obtained for the disclosure and the contemplated purposes.** Transfer of personal data outside Singapore is subject to additional requirements, including ensuring the receiving jurisdiction has privacy safeguards of equivalent standard. A general common law duty of confidence also applies alongside the PDPA.
- Source: [Baker McKenzie Resource Hub, Singapore insurance guide](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/asia-pacific-insurance/asia-pacific/singapore/topics/guide-for-insurance-sales-advisory-and-distribution).

**[INFERENCE] The awkward question you will be asked, and the answer.** *"The SME consents. But the SME's 14 employees named in the WICA schedule didn't consent to you."* The workable answers, in order of strength:
1. **Don't hold their data** — redact at ingestion, keep aggregates. Strongest answer.
2. **Act as data intermediary** for the SME or the intermediary, under a written contract, so the controller's existing consents and notifications carry the processing. See below.
3. Rely on a PDPA consent exception. **[UNVERIFIED]** — I did not verify which exceptions in the PDPA First Schedule would apply. Do not assert an exception you have not had confirmed.

### Data intermediary status — the most useful concept for your architecture [VERIFIED]

Under the PDPA, an organisation that engages a **data intermediary** to process personal data on its behalf remains responsible for ensuring the processing complies with the PDPA.

Where a contractor processes personal data **as a data intermediary pursuant to a contract in writing**, the contractor is **not subject to the data protection obligations in Parts III to VI of the PDPA, except for the obligations relating to protection and retention of personal data.**
- Sources: [PDPC — Guide on Data Protection Clauses for Agreements Relating to the Processing of Personal Data, 1 Feb 2021](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Resource-for-Organisation/Guide-on-Data-Protection-Clauses-for-Agreements-Relating-to-the-Processing-of-Personal-Data-1-Feb-2021.pdf?la=en); [PDPC — Guide to Managing Data Intermediaries (2020)](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Other-Guides/Guide-to-Managing-Data-Intermediaries--2020.pdf?ref=sorena.io); [PDPC DPO Connect — Data Intermediaries](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/New_DPO_Connect/feb_16/pdf/DataIntermedieries.pdf).

**This is the single most valuable regulatory fact in Section B for your architecture.** **[INFERENCE]** If you contract in writing as a **data intermediary** to the SME (or to the broker, or to Zurich), your PDPA surface reduces to **protection** and **retention** — the two obligations you were going to have to meet anyway to be credible. Consent, notification, purpose limitation, access and correction stay with the controller who already has the customer relationship. Architect for this deliberately: written data processing agreement, controller-directed processing only, no independent use of the data for your own purposes. If you train models on customer documents you break this, so decide early.

The PDPC's Guide to Managing Data Intermediaries covers the DI management lifecycle — governance and risk assessment, policies and practices, service management, exit management — and includes an annex on developing contract clauses. **[INFERENCE]** Read it and pre-build the clauses. Zurich's procurement will ask for exactly these.

### Data Protection Officer [VERIFIED]

**Section 11(3) of the PDPA** provides that an organisation **shall designate one or more individuals to be responsible for ensuring that the organisation complies with the PDPA.** The PDPC calls this person the **Data Protection Officer (DPO)**, though that phrase does not itself appear in the Act.
- Sources: [PDPC — Advisory Guidelines on Key Concepts, Chapter 20, The Openness Obligation](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Advisory-Guidelines/the-openness-obligation---ch-20-(270717).pdf), which states section 11(3)'s effect directly; [PDPC — Fundamentals of the PDPA course flyer, 28 Oct 2025](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/resource-for-organisation/fundamentals-of-personal-data-protection-act_28oct2025.pdf), stating organisations are required to appoint one or more DPOs; [Recording Law — PDPA compliance for businesses](https://www.recordinglaw.com/singapore/data-protection/pdpa-for-businesses/), noting the DPO label is the PDPC's rather than the Act's.
- PDPC has also stated plainly that all businesses, big or small, need a DPO. Source: [PDPC business flyer](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Resource-for-Organisation/pdpc_b2b_ad1_final---20-may-16.pdf).
- The **Openness Obligation** requires organisations to develop and implement data protection policies and practices and to make information about them available. Source: Chapter 20 above; [PDPC — Guide to Notification](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Other-Guides/Guide-to-Notification-260919.pdf?la=en).
- **Data breach notification:** where a breach is likely to cause significant harm to affected individuals, **or affects 500 or more individuals**, notification to the PDPC is legally required. Source: [Singapore Police Force — Ransomware advisory](https://www.police.gov.sg/Advisories/Cybercrime/Ransomware). **[SECONDARY]** for the 500 threshold — it appears on an SPF advisory rather than a PDPC page in my results; confirm against the PDPA breach notification provisions before citing the number.

**[INFERENCE] Practical, cheap, and worth a bullet.** Appointing a named DPO costs you nothing and is a legal requirement from day one, including pre-revenue. A slide bullet reading "DPO appointed; data protection policy published; written DI agreements in place; breach notification runbook tested" is disproportionately reassuring to this audience.

### Additional PDPA-adjacent points [VERIFIED]

- Retention limitation is a distinct obligation. Source: [PDPC Advisory Guidelines for the Education Sector, 25 Apr 2024](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/advisory-guidelines-for-education-sector_25-apr-2024.pdf) (used here only as a source confirming the obligation exists as a named obligation).
- Access and correction obligations sit with the controller. Source: [PDPC Advisory Guidelines on Key Concepts, Chapter 15](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-key-concepts/chapter-15-9-oct-2019.pdf).
- PDPC provides a free **PDPA Online Assessment Tool for Organisations (PATO)** to help improve data protection policies and practices. Source: [PDPC course flyer](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/resource-for-organisation/fundamentals-of-personal-data-protection-act_28oct2025.pdf). **[INFERENCE]** Run it. It is free and gives you something concrete to reference.

## B6. MAS regulatory sandbox and Sandbox Express — and whether you need one

### What exists [VERIFIED / SECONDARY]

MAS operates **three sandbox pathways**: **Sandbox**, **Sandbox Plus**, and **Sandbox Express**, each offering tailored regulatory relief allowing FIs and technology firms to experiment with innovative services in a live but controlled environment.
- Source: [Global Law Experts — How to apply for MAS Fintech Sandbox](https://globallawexperts.com/how-to-apply-for-mas-fintech-sandbox/), 2026. **[SECONDARY]**

**Sandbox Express** is a pre-defined, faster route available only where the business model's risks are already well understood; it does not remove financial stability requirements but softens certain reporting and infrastructure obligations for a limited period. Applications are assessed on **technological innovativeness of the financial service** and **fitness and propriety of the applicant's key stakeholders**, with **approval within 21 days**.
- Sources: [Pinsent Masons — MAS outlines new regulatory sandbox for fintech](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/en-gb/out-law/news/mas-outlines-new-regulatory-fintech-sandbox) (21-day approval, two assessment criteria); [YB Case on Sandbox Express](https://ybcase.com/en/fintech/bystryj-zapusk-finteh-produkta-v-singapure-cerez-sandbox-express-mas), 2026. **[SECONDARY]**

The general sandbox operates within **agreed boundaries set by MAS**, including limits on the number of users onboarded and caps on transaction volumes, with measurement and monitoring throughout. Source: [YB Case on MAS Regulatory Sandbox](https://ybcase.com/en/fintech/zapusk-finteh-proekta-v-singapure-cerez-mas-regulatory-sandbox), 2026. **[SECONDARY]**

An important framing point from MAS's own original consultation: FIs are **already free to launch new financial products, services or processes without first seeking MAS's permission**, provided they have carried out due diligence and there is no breach of legal and regulatory requirements. Source: [MAS consultation paper, as reproduced by Taiwan NDC](https://report.ndc.gov.tw/ReportFront/PageSystem/reportFileDownload/C10600154/006). **[SECONDARY]**

**Access note [UNVERIFIED]:** `mas.gov.sg/development/fintech/regulatory-sandbox` returned a maintenance page during this research. **Before submission, pull the current MAS sandbox page yourself** — sandbox parameters and Sandbox Plus grant terms change, and a wrong sandbox claim in front of MAS-adjacent judges is a self-inflicted wound.

### Do you need a sandbox? **Almost certainly not. [INFERENCE, high confidence]**

The reasoning, which is also your answer if asked:
1. **A sandbox relieves you of regulatory requirements you would otherwise breach.** On the B1 analysis, a general insurance diagnostic that does not advise on investment products, does not arrange or solicit insurance, and does not handle premium, is not subject to a licensing requirement to be relieved of. **There is nothing to sandbox.**
2. **MAS's own starting point is permissive** — no prior permission needed to launch, subject to due diligence and no breach of requirements.
3. **A pre-incorporation team cannot realistically use it anyway.** Sandbox Express assesses fitness and propriety of key stakeholders and presumes a defined regulated activity. Both are hard without an incorporated entity, and neither is your bottleneck.
4. **The opportunity cost is real.** Sandbox application effort competes directly with building the accuracy harness and the PDPA architecture, which is what actually unblocks a Zurich pilot.

**What to say if asked "shouldn't you be in the sandbox?"**
> *"We looked at it. A sandbox relieves a licensing requirement — on our analysis, a general insurance diagnostic that outputs findings rather than recommendations and never touches premium doesn't attract one. MAS's own position is that firms can launch without prior permission provided they've done the diligence and aren't in breach. So the sandbox isn't our constraint. Our constraints are PDPA and Zurich's third-party AI controls, and we've built for both."*

**[INFERENCE]** That answer demonstrates you understand what a sandbox is *for*, which is a better signal than having applied to one.

### One caveat where a sandbox conversation becomes relevant [INFERENCE]

If your roadmap includes **quote-and-bind**, **premium collection**, **commission**, or **any life or health product**, the analysis changes and an early conversation with MAS FinTech — not necessarily a sandbox application — becomes worthwhile. Say this proactively. It shows you've mapped the perimeter of your own roadmap, not just your MVP.

**[INFERENCE]** The QBE and Straits Times finding in Section D — that most Singapore SMEs buy the insurance that regulation or a contract forces them to buy, and nothing more — means the *product* problem is not persuasion. It is timing. An SME will not read a coverage report because you sent one. It will read one when a landlord, a bank, a government tender, a licensing authority or MOM is about to ask it a question it cannot answer. Every row below is a moment when an SME is already looking at its insurance. **That is your distribution.**

The table is ordered by strength of compulsion: statutory first, then contractual, then commercial.

**Three important qualifiers on the PI maximum that you should carry, because an underwriter will know them:**
1. **The maximum applies only to total PI (100% PI).** Where PI% is lower, both minimum and maximum are **prorated**.
2. The stated amounts **exclude the additional 25%** paid for total PI to offset the injured employee's cost of care. MOM's guidance confirms: if a doctor or dentist awards 100% PI, an additional 25% is added on top of the compensation amount.
3. Compensation is computed as **average monthly earnings × age multiplying factor × % permanent incapacity**, with the min/max acting as a collar, not as the formula.
- Source: [MOM press release annex footnote 1](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2024/0802-higher-compensation-limits-under-the-work-injury-compensation-act) and [MOM types of compensation](https://www.mom.gov.sg/workplace-safety-and-health/work-injury-compensation/types-of-compensation).

### WICA — penalties and the under-declaration trap [VERIFIED]

- **Failure to provide adequate insurance is an offence carrying a fine of up to $10,000 or imprisonment of up to 12 months, or both.**
- **Under-declaring the number of employees to the insurer causes some employees to be uninsured, and MOM warns all claims may be affected by wrongful declaration.**
- Employers must ensure the nature of occupation and other insurer-requested information is accurately reflected, **otherwise they may not be fully covered**.
- **Work-related exclusion clauses are no longer allowed** in WIC policies except for asbestos-related cases; the insurer is liable even if the policy contains such an exclusion, **but may still recover from the employer where a recovery clause exists**.
- For employees outside the compulsory categories, the employer may choose whether to insure — **but if those employees make a valid claim, the employer must compensate them regardless of whether they are insured.**
- Required information at confirmation: **UEN, nature of business, aggregated headcount, aggregated annual wages, occupation of employees.** MOM publishes single-entity and multiple-entity standard declaration forms.
- Coverage-gap risk MOM specifically flags: injury occurring **after an old WIC policy expired and before a valid new one started**.
- **Project WIC policies:** no new project WIC policies from **1 September 2020**. Employers must hold their own WIC insurance even where employees are covered by a project policy, because **project policies do not cover some instances of work injury** — MOM gives the examples of injury in company transport or while working outside the project site. Employers relying only on a project policy **may be charged for non-insurance**.
- Insurer payment deadline: **within 21 days from the date of service on the notice of assessment or notice of computation.**
- All sources: [MOM — Work injury compensation insurance](http://www.mom.gov.sg/workplace-safety-and-health/work-injury-compensation/work-injury-compensation-insurance); [MOM — Types of compensation](https://www.mom.gov.sg/workplace-safety-and-health/work-injury-compensation/types-of-compensation); [MOM — Must my WIC policy be issued by a designated insurer?](https://www.mom.gov.sg/faq/work-injury-compensation/must-my-wic-insurance-policy-be-issued-by-a-designated-insurer); [MOM — Do I need WIC insurance for employees already covered by existing policies?](https://mom.gov.sg/faq/work-injury-compensation/do-i-need-to-buy-wic-insurance-for-employees-who-are-already-covered-by-existing-policies).

**[INFERENCE] — this is the best single product feature in the entire dossier.**

MOM has told you, in writing, exactly what an SME gets wrong: **it under-declares headcount and mis-states occupational class to its WIC insurer, and it relies on a project policy that doesn't cover company transport or off-site work.** Both defects are detectable by comparing three documents an SME already has — the WIC policy schedule, the payroll or CPF submission, and the work pass list. Both defects invalidate cover in a way the SME will not discover until a claim. And MOM itself publishes the standard declaration form that defines the correct answer.

That is a coverage-gap diagnostic with an objective ground truth, a statutory consequence, a $10,000-and-12-months penalty attached, and an annual forced-attention event (renewal, 21 days before commencement). If you build one feature, build this one, and put MOM's own warning language on the slide.

### Caveats on rows I could not fully verify — read this before building the table into a slide

**Row 7 (BCA CRS) — insurance as a CRS condition: [UNVERIFIED].** BCA's CRS page describes Specific Registration Requirements covering **financial, track record, personnel and other requirements (e.g. certifications)**. **SMEs are enterprises with operating revenue of not more than $100 million OR employment of not more than 200 workers.** Note the **OR** — it is disjunctive, so a firm qualifies on either limb.

3. **Design as a findings engine used by intermediaries, not a recommendation engine used by SMEs.** Three independent lines of evidence converge: the FAA perimeter is safest when you state facts rather than recommend products (B1); 65% of Singapore SMEs still buy offline with agent share rising (D4); and Zurich Singapore distributes through brokers and created a broker leadership role in 2025 (A1). The same design decision satisfies regulation, market behaviour and your customer's distribution model.

4. **Make PDPA architecture a headline feature, not a compliance appendix.** SME AI-privacy concern went from 10% to 66% in one year (D4), and you are asking those firms to upload documents containing employee and director data. Redaction at ingestion, Singapore data residency, written data intermediary agreements limiting you to protection and retention obligations (B5), a named DPO, and no training on customer documents. This is a conversion feature disguised as governance.

5. **Bring evidence, not intentions, on AI safety.** Run the IMDA GenAI Starter Kit's five risk tests — hallucination and inaccuracy, bias, undesirable content, data leakage, adversarial prompt vulnerability — on your prototype and show the results. Then mirror the four things Zurich itself said made Sixfold work: start where the human already knows the answer, build an accuracy harness that reports back to Zurich, name the governance framework you meet, publish a hard number. Add a one-page sub-processor register, because under MAS's proposed TPRM Guidelines your sub-processors become Zurich's regulatory disclosure (B4b). That package is a procurement-ready pilot, and almost no hackathon team will arrive with it.
